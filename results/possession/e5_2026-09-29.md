# E5: sequences on the new possession state (29 Sep 2026)

**Problem (from E4):** the new "who has the ball" state flips to the other team about 190 times per 5 minutes when the ball
sits between two players. Every flip cut a sequence, so we counted 3x too many (SFK-BP 64 -> 210, median 0.6 s).

**Fix (in `P.sequences`, the state is not touched):**
- the other team only takes over once it has held the ball 0.5 s more than the owner (counted from its first touch, which becomes the start of its sequence);
- a team's sequence carries on through loose-ball gaps up to 3 s (was 1 s).

The old model (`IPANEMA_POSSESSION=viterbi`) keeps the old rule, so switching back gives exactly the old numbers.

**Answer key:** Metrica's 2 free pro games (4 halves, 694 real sequences = chains of passes/carries/shots by one team,
ended by the other team or the ball going out). A real sequence counts as found if one of ours (same team) starts within 2 s.

| Metrica, 4 halves | ours | real ones found | ours that are real |
|---|---|---|---|
| clean positions, old model | 517 | 41% | 56% |
| clean, new state, E4 rule | 1562 | 76% | 34% |
| **clean, new state, E5 rule** | **567** | **59%** | **72%** |
| noisy (like our Veo tracking), old model | 331 | 10% | 22% |

Other settings tried are in `results/possession/e5_2026-09-29.json`. A stricter rule (1 s takeover) gets closer to the
old counts but finds fewer real sequences. 0.5 s + 3 s was the best balance on both clean and noisy data.

**Nightly Metrica check switched** to the pipeline's state (`P.pipeline_state`, same as `ipanema/run.py`): set pieces
from the old model's dead-ball runs, lost balls with the 1 s rule, plus a new sequences row. Local dry run of
`tools/nightly_quick.py`: passes worst half 15% off (was 17%), possession 8 pts off (was 13), sequences 47% found /
46% real. The scorecard has a note that numbers before 29 Sep are from the old model.
